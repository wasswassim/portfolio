Important notice

This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Admission rules, fees and deadlines change every year, so always check the official call of your university and Universitaly before applying or paying anything.

You live in Tunisia and want a place at a university in Rome. Where do you apply, and in what order?

Short answer: you apply to the university first, on its own portal. Once it admits you, you fill in the Universitaly pre-enrolment, which the university validates and sends to the Italian Embassy in Tunis for your study visa. The visa deadline for 2027/28 Bachelor's and Master's courses is 31 October 2027.

This is part 2 of our guide to studying in Italy for Tunisian students, which sets out the whole path and the 2027/28 calendar. Here we cover choosing a course, applying to the five main universities in Rome, Universitaly and the PhD.

1. How admission works for a non-EU student living in Tunisia

You count as a non-EU student resident abroad who needs a visa. The Ministry of University (MUR) circular, valid for 2026/27 and 2027/28, sets two separate steps:

  1. The university application: on the university's own portal, with its fee and, for some courses, a test. The university decides whether to admit you.
  2. The Universitaly pre-enrolment: once admitted, you fill in a form on Universitaly. The university checks and validates it, and the file reaches the Embassy in Tunis. It is mandatory for every visa applicant, PhD included.

Each university sets its own dates, so there is no single national deadline. Medicine has new access rules (Law 26/2025) whose details for international students are not yet published, so it is not covered here.

2. Find a course and check the entry requirements

Open Universitaly's course search↗ (opens in a new tab) and filter by city (Roma), degree type (Laurea for a Bachelor's, Laurea Magistrale for a Master's) and language of teaching. Then open the course on the university's site, where the call, fee and deadlines are. For each course, check:

  • Your diploma: a Bachelor's needs a secondary diploma after at least 12 years of school, which the Tunisian bac meets. A Master's needs a Bachelor's degree and transcripts. Some courses add an aptitude test.
  • The language: Italian-taught courses use an Italian test at B2 or above organised by the university (an Italian B2 certificate exempts you). English-taught courses need a B2 certificate in English.
  • Your documents: diplomas must be legalised and translated, and the university may ask for a DOV or CIMEA statement. See how to legalise your documents and DOV or CIMEA.

3. Apply to Sapienza, Tor Vergata or Roma Tre

Each public university uses its own portal. Dates below are from the 2026/27 calls; 2027/28 is expected to follow a similar pattern, so check the 2027/28 call.

Sapienza: MoveIn, then Infostud

  1. Check the requirementsRead Sapienza admissions↗ (opens in a new tab) and the 2026/27 academic requirements (PDF)↗ (opens in a new tab). Some English-taught Bachelor's courses ask for a SAT score of 960 or more, or a TOLC.
  2. Apply on MoveInCreate an account on MoveIn↗ (opens in a new tab), upload your documents and pick your course. Each application costs EUR 30 (no waiver), with a maximum of 2; the eligibility check costs EUR 10.
  3. Watch the windowIn 2026/27 it ran from 22 December 2025 to 15 May 2026 for non-EU visa applicants. For 2027/28 it is expected to open around December 2026 (check the 2027/28 call).
  4. After admissionDo the Universitaly pre-enrolment (deadline 30 June in 2026), then register on Infostud↗ (opens in a new tab). Questions: recruitment@uniroma1.it.

Tor Vergata: Delphi and one call per course

  1. Find your course's callStart from Tor Vergata admissions↗ (opens in a new tab). Each course has its own deadline and fee: in 2026/27, the MSc in Economics closed on 29 May (EUR 30) and EEBL on 28 May (EUR 50).
  2. Apply on DelphiRegister on Delphi↗ (opens in a new tab), upload your documents and pay the fee.
  3. UniversitalyIn 2026 the pre-enrolment was due by 31 July, and before the test for courses with one. Questions: international.students@uniroma2.it.

Roma Tre: GOMP and Universitaly together

  1. Read the guideDownload the 2026/27 how-to-apply guide (PDF)↗ (opens in a new tab) and read the international admissions page↗ (opens in a new tab).
  2. Apply on GOMP and UniversitalyRegister on GOMP↗ (opens in a new tab) and upload the same documents there and on Universitaly (deadline 15 September in 2026).
  3. Italian testFor Italian-taught courses, book the Italian B2 test through GOMP unless you hold a B2 certificate. Questions: international.admissions@uniroma3.it.

Public tuition depends on income and country (Sapienza's flat fee for Tunisia, band A, is EUR 300 a year). See the costs in part 1 and the DiSCo scholarship.

4. Private universities: LUISS and LUMSA

LUISS: the Luiss Test

Non-EU students enter through the Luiss Test or international certifications (LUISS page for non-EU students↗ (opens in a new tab)). For 2027/28: applications from October 2026 to 10 February 2027, test from 22 to 26 February 2027, enrolment by 4 May 2027, fee EUR 150. First-year Bachelor's tuition is a flat EUR 15,000 a year plus the regional tax. There are scholarships for non-EU students (20 full waivers in 2025/26), but the 2027/28 call is not out yet.

LUMSA: Italian-taught Bachelor's courses

LUMSA's Bachelor's courses are taught in Italian (B2 needed) and start with an online admission test (EUR 100). For a Master's, use LUMSA's apply page↗ (opens in a new tab) (also EUR 100). Students resident abroad pay a flat EUR 4,390 a year (2026/27 student guide, PDF↗ (opens in a new tab)). At both private universities, you still do the Universitaly pre-enrolment after admission.

6. The Universitaly pre-enrolment, step by step

The pre-enrolment is not a second application: it is the form that links your admission to your visa.

  1. Create your accountRegister on Universitaly↗ (opens in a new tab) with your name exactly as in your passport, in Latin letters, no accents. Use an email you check: the visa appointment arrives there.
  2. Fill in the formChoose the university and the course you were admitted to (one course per pre-enrolment), the Italian Embassy in Tunis, and study as the purpose of stay.
  3. Upload and submitUpload your passport, diploma, transcripts and language certificate, prepared as in our documents guide. Submit and keep the summary for the visa.

The university then validates your file. New since 2026/27: each university can validate only its reserved places for foreign students plus 20%, then Universitaly blocks further validations, so apply early. After validation, ALMAVIVA emails you a date: see the visa appointment. The 2027/28 visa deadline is 31 October 2027. More answers in the Universitaly FAQ (PDF)↗ (opens in a new tab).

7. Applying for a PhD in Rome

Each university publishes one PhD call a year, usually in spring. The 2026 calls (42nd cycle) closed on 9 June (Tor Vergata), 17 June (Sapienza) and 14 July (Roma Tre). The 43rd cycle (2027/28) is expected in spring 2027 (check each call):

  • Sapienza PhD admissions↗ (opens in a new tab)
  • Tor Vergata PhD calls↗ (opens in a new tab)
  • Roma Tre PhD call↗ (opens in a new tab)

You apply with your Master's degree and transcripts. Once selected, you still do the Universitaly pre-enrolment; there is no fixed visa date, but apply before teaching starts.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply to several Italian universities?

Yes, each university has its own application and fee (Sapienza allows 2 applications). But one Universitaly pre-enrolment covers one course, so you pick one once admitted.

Is Universitaly the same as applying to the university?

No. You apply on the university's portal first; Universitaly is the pre-enrolment the university validates for your visa.

Do I need the SAT to study in Rome?

Only for some courses: at Sapienza, some English-taught Bachelor's courses ask for a SAT score of 960 or more, or a TOLC.

Can I study in English in Rome?

Yes, some courses are taught in English. You need a B2 certificate in English; use the language filter on Universitaly's course search.
