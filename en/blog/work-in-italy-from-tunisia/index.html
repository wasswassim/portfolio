Working in Italy as a Tunisian: The Training-Abroad Route

By Wassim GatriPublished October 7, 2026Updated October 7, 20267 min readLast verified October 7, 2026Guide ↓

Important notice

This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Immigration programs, requirements and procedures can change, so always check the official sources before applying or paying anything.

Looking for a legal way to work in Italy from Tunisia?

There is a special mechanism under Italian immigration law that allows, in specific cases, foreign workers who have completed approved vocational, language and civic training programs in their home country to enter Italy to work outside the Decreto Flussi quotas.

But one essential point needs to be clear: this is not an open work visa for every Tunisian, and it does not mean the Italian government gives every applicant a job contract automatically. You must meet the requirements of an approved program, and then the hiring procedure and the Nulla Osta request follow the legal route.

1. What is this route?

The Italian immigration law, the Testo Unico Immigrazione, regulates a special route for certain foreign workers who completed vocational, language and civic training in their home country, provided the programs are approved under Italian rules.

The key advantage of this route is that workers who meet its conditions can enter Italy to work outside the Decreto Flussi quotas.

The route can be simplified as follows: approved training in Tunisia → hiring, or matching the worker with an employer → Nulla Osta request from the Italian employer → work visa → travel to Italy → residence and work procedures.

2. Who can use this route?

This route does not automatically apply to every Tunisian who wants to work in Italy.

A person must be part of an approved training program and meet the conditions set by that program.

There is no single fixed list saying this route is always limited to six occupations. Occupations and sectors depend on the programs that are approved and currently open.

So the right question is not: which jobs are in demand in Italy? It is: which approved training programs are currently open to Tunisians, and is there one that fits my occupation and training?

3. Which programs and occupations are available to Tunisians?

Programs change over time. New programs may be added and existing ones may end.

There are Tunisia-linked programs in several sectors, including construction and public works, tourism and hospitality, mechanics and maintenance, restaurants, as well as other industrial and service sectors.

One well-known project is THAMM Plus, a cooperation program that aims to train and integrate Tunisian workers in Italy, especially in the construction sector.

A program appearing on the official list does not mean applications are open right now. It may be at the launch stage, under way, or its application period may have ended. So always check the current announcement and the program's status with the official source.

4. How do you start from Tunisia?

Step 1: Follow the official channels

Do not start by buying a work contract from an intermediary on Facebook or WhatsApp. Watch for job and training programs published through ANETI and the other official bodies taking part in the program.

For some Italy-related job offers, applicants must register with their local employment office and then use the international employment system to apply.

ANETI International: aneti-international.tn

Step 2: Make sure your occupation matches the offer's requirements

Each offer sets its own requirements. You may be asked, for example, for a vocational training certificate, a certificate of competence, documented work experience, a professional driving licence for some jobs, or other requirements tied to the occupation or the program. Do not rely on a general list posted on social media; read the official offer itself.

Step 3: Pass the tests and interviews

In some projects, once initial applications are accepted, applicants go through an interview and/or a practical trade test. The test may be directly tied to the occupation, especially for technical and skilled-trade jobs.

5. Which documents should you prepare?

It is best to prepare your file in advance, because the application window for some job offers is short.

  • National identity card: a clear copy, still valid.
  • Passport: make sure it is valid.
  • Vocational certificate or the required training or competence certificate.
  • Previous work certificates, especially when experience is part of the offer's requirements.
  • Proof of work experience, such as official documents showing employment dates when they are required.
  • Criminal record certificate when requested.
  • Professional driving licence for some transport and driving jobs.

Important

This is not a single list for all programs. The final documents depend on the announcement and the program you apply to.

6. How does the training work?

Training is the core of this route.

A program can include vocational training, Italian language, civic training, and occupational safety basics.

The length of training varies from one program to another, so no single duration should be presented as a general rule for all programs.

7. What happens after the training is completed?

After finishing the qualifying program, the hiring stage and the procedures for entering Italy begin.

The Italian employer is the one who files the Nulla Osta request on the worker's behalf. The worker does not file a Nulla Osta request independently.

For workers who met the conditions of the training-abroad route, entry procedures follow the system specific to this route, outside the Decreto Flussi quotas.

8. What is the Nulla Osta?

Nulla Osta al lavoro is the authorization required to complete the process of a foreign worker entering Italy to work.

In simple terms: the worker is qualified → there is an Italian employer → the employer files the Nulla Osta request → after approval, the visa stage begins.

Warning

Do not pay anyone who sells you a “ready-made Nulla Osta”. You must know who the employer is and which official channel the request is filed through.

9. How do you apply for the work visa from Tunisia?

Once the Nulla Osta is issued, the visa application stage begins. For residents of Tunisia, rely on the information published by the Italian Embassy in Tunisia and the authorized body that processes visa applications.

For a long stay for work, the visa is a National Visa D, depending on the nature of the case.

Visa information: Italian Embassy in Tunisia: visas

10. When must the visa application be filed?

For workers who completed training programs abroad, there are special rules setting the period in which the visa application can be filed after training ends. According to the current information for this route, the period extends to 12 months from the end of training, subject to the stated conditions and procedures.

11. What happens after arriving in Italy?

Arriving in Italy does not mean the procedures are over. You must complete the procedures for the Contratto di soggiorno and then the Permesso di soggiorno, according to the procedures that apply to your case.

For this route, the Contratto di soggiorno must be completed within 15 days of entering Italy under the current rules.

Always keep copies of applications, receipts, appointments, correspondence and official documents.

12. What is SIISL?

There is an important update in 2026 that job seekers should not miss. SIISL is the Sistema Informativo per l'Inclusione Sociale e Lavorativa, an Italian system that aims to support access to inclusion and job opportunities.

Procedures have been set for registering, in this system, foreign citizens who completed training programs in their home country under this route. This is a recent addition, so you may find older guides online that do not mention SIISL.

13. Beware of scams

Warning

If someone says to you: “Pay me and I will get you a work contract in Italy” — stop. An Italian or EU logo on an image or post does not prove the offer is official.

Always check with ANETI, the Tunisian Ministry of Employment, the Italian Ministry of Labour, the Italian Ministry of the Interior, and the Italian Embassy in Tunisia.

The Tunisian Ministry of Employment and Vocational Training warns about fake job offers and unlawful bodies that ask job seekers for money for work abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Tunisian work in Italy outside the Decreto Flussi?

Yes, in specific cases, through the route for workers who completed qualifying training programs abroad.

Is this route open to every Tunisian?

No. You must meet the requirements of a qualifying program.

Do I need an employer?

Yes. At the Nulla Osta stage, there must be an Italian employer who files the nominative request.

Can I buy a work contract?

No. Deal only with official channels, and treat anyone who asks for money in exchange for a guaranteed contract or visa as a warning sign.

Are all occupations eligible?

No. It depends on the approved programs and the sectors and occupations they cover.

Do I need to know Italian?

This type of program includes language training, and the detailed requirements vary by program.

How long does the training course take?

There is no single fixed duration for all programs. It varies by program, sector and occupation.

What do I do after arriving in Italy?

You complete the Contratto di soggiorno and Permesso di soggiorno procedures, according to the rules and deadlines that apply to your case.
