Important notice

This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Work rules for permit holders and the platforms' own rules can change, so check the official sources linked below before you sign a contract.

You have your residence permit for study and want a part-time job in Rome. How many hours are you allowed, and where do you actually find work?

The short answer: you can work as an employee for up to 20 hours a week and 1,040 hours a year, with a regular, declared contract. In Rome, most student jobs are in cafés, bars and restaurants, and a free ad on Subito saying you are looking for work is often the quickest way in.

This is part 3 of our settling-in series, after the bank account and the residenza. For the basics of your first month, read the student work section of our arrival guide.

1. Before you start: SIM, IBAN and codice fiscale

  • An Italian SIM card. Subito asks you to verify your account by SMS and only accepts phone numbers from EU countries and Switzerland, so a +216 number will not work.
  • An IBAN. Employers pay salaries by bank transfer. See how to open a bank account in Italy as a student.
  • Your codice fiscale. It normally comes with your permesso application through the Questura; if you need it earlier, request it at any Agenzia delle Entrate office with form AA4/8.
  • Your residenza helps with many local procedures; see how to register your residenza in Rome.

Show the permit receipt to employers

Until your permit card arrives, give employers and agencies a copy of the receipt (ricevuta) with your passport: it shows you applied for a study permit and lets them register a regular contract.

2. How many hours can you work on a student permit?

The study permit allows employee work (lavoro subordinato) for up to 20 hours a week, which can be spread over 52 weeks, within an annual limit of 1,040 hours (DPR 394/1999, art. 14, c. 4↗ (opens in a new tab)).

In its note of 24 May 2022, the Ispettorato Nazionale del Lavoro made it clear that 20 hours is a weekly ceiling, not an average: a contract above 20 hours a week is not compliant even if your yearly total stays under 1,040. You cannot work 35 hours in August and make up for it later. To work more, you must convert your permit to a work permit, before it expires.

Keep your exams on track

Working does not block your renewal, but exams do. Study permits are renewed if you passed one exam in your first year and at least two in each later year, and they cannot be issued for more than 3 years beyond the length of your course (DPR 394/1999, art. 46↗ (opens in a new tab)). A job that wrecks your exam record puts your permit at risk.

3. Find a café or restaurant job on Subito

Subito↗ (opens in a new tab) is Italy's big classifieds site, and Rome has plenty of café, bar and restaurant jobs on it. Instead of only answering employer ads, post your own ad in Lavoro e servizi > Candidati in cerca di lavoro, the section for job seekers. Private users get 20 free ads there, and Subito refuses job offers that ask candidates for money (Subito rules↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Step 1: create your account

Sign up on the website or the Subito app with your email, then confirm your Italian phone number with the SMS code (phone verification↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Step 2: choose the job-seeker category

Tap Inserisci annuncio and pick Lavoro e servizi, then Candidati in cerca di lavoro. You can browse the Rome job-seeker ads↗ (opens in a new tab) first to see how others write theirs.

Step 3: write a short, clear ad

  • Title: the role and the area, for example “Cameriere / barista part-time, Roma San Lorenzo”.
  • Languages: Arabic, French and English, plus your honest Italian level.
  • Availability: the days and times you can work, within 20 hours a week.
  • Area: the neighbourhood you live in and how far you can travel.
  • Photo: optional; a clear, friendly one can help.
  • Contact: ask employers to write to you through the app chat.

Step 4: answer quickly and keep the job legal

In my experience and that of my friends, calls and offers usually arrived within a week of posting. That is experience, not a guarantee. When someone calls, ask for a written contract with your weekly hours before your first shift.

4. Apply on Indeed Italy

On it.indeed.com↗ (opens in a new tab), search for “part time studenti” with Roma as the location. Upload your CV or build one on Indeed (upload a CV↗ (opens in a new tab)); some jobs let you apply directly with Indeed Apply, others send you to the employer's site. Set a job alert so new offers reach your inbox (job alerts↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Check the weekly hours

Many “student” ads in Rome ask for 25 to 30 hours a week, above your legal cap. Read the hours in each ad before you apply.

5. Temp agencies with a branch in Rome

Temp agencies (agenzie per il lavoro) place people in shops, warehouses, hotels and events. By law, authorised agencies cannot charge workers any fee (D.Lgs. 276/2003, art. 11↗ (opens in a new tab)). You can check any agency on the Ministry of Labour register↗ (opens in a new tab).

Bring your CV, permit (or receipt) and codice fiscale, and tell them upfront that you can work at most 20 hours a week.

6. University jobs: 150-ore collaborations and job placement

Universities pay students for part-time campus work, the 150-ore collaborations (borse di collaborazione). Sapienza's 2025/26 call offered 250 places, paying EUR 1,295 for 150 hours, tax-free. It is not an employment contract, and it required Italian B2 and English B2 (Sapienza call 2025/26↗ (opens in a new tab)). The 2025/26 deadline has passed, so watch for the new call in autumn.

Good news if you have a grant: the Lazio DiSCo call says its scholarship is compatible with 150-ore collaborations; see the DiSCo scholarship guide.

  • Sapienza: Career Service job board on JobTeaser and internships on TSP (opportunities↗ (opens in a new tab)).
  • Tor Vergata: placement office↗ (opens in a new tab) and the job board at uniroma2.jobteaser.com.
  • Roma Tre: the Job Placement office↗ (opens in a new tab).
  • Porta Futuro Lazio, run by DiSCo, has desks at Sapienza, Roma Tre and Tor Vergata: free CV checks, guidance and courses (portafuturolazio.it↗ (opens in a new tab)).

7. How to spot a job scam in Rome

The Polizia Postale warned about fake job offers on 19 January 2024↗ (opens in a new tab) and about the WhatsApp “task scam” on 30 January 2025↗ (opens in a new tab). ANPAL also flagged fake offers using the EURES logo↗ (opens in a new tab). Walk away if an offer:

  • Asks you to pay anything: a course, a kit, a registration fee.
  • Wants a copy of your passport, permit or your bank codes before any interview.
  • Arrives on WhatsApp from an unknown number and pays you to “like” posts or write reviews.
  • Promises pay far above normal for very easy work.
  • Comes without a written contract, or offers cash in hand.
  • Pushes you to leave Subito or Indeed and click an outside link.

Report scams to the Polizia Postale online office↗ (opens in a new tab) or any police station.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours can I work on a student permit in Italy?

Up to 20 hours a week as an employee, with a limit of 1,040 hours a year. The 20 hours are a weekly ceiling, not an average, so you cannot work more one week and less the next.

How do I find a job in a café or restaurant in Rome?

Post a free ad in Subito's “Candidati in cerca di lavoro” section with the role, your area, your languages and your availability. Also answer employer ads on Subito and Indeed.

Do temp agencies in Italy charge a fee?

No. Italian law (D.Lgs. 276/2003, art. 11) forbids authorised agencies from charging workers, so any agency that asks you for money is a red flag.

Can I work with the DiSCo scholarship?

Yes for 150-ore university collaborations: the DiSCo call says they are compatible with the scholarship. For other jobs, check the income rules in the current DiSCo call.

Can I sign up on Subito with a Tunisian phone number?

No. Subito's SMS verification only accepts numbers from EU countries and Switzerland, so get an Italian SIM first.

What if I want to work more than 20 hours a week?

You must convert your study permit to a work permit, before it expires. Until then, keep every contract at 20 hours a week or less.
