You have just arrived in Rome. Which bank account should you open to get an IBAN for rent, DiSCo and a salary, and what will the bank ask you for?

The short answer: in my experience and that of other students, Intesa Sanpaolo is the best first choice. I opened a normal current account (about EUR 6 a month) with only my codice fiscale, my passport and proof of enrolment. Revolut is the fast alternative in the app, Wise is the easy backup, and the conto di base is a legal right if a bank says no.

This is part 1 of the series on settling in Rome after you arrive. If you still have to pay fees before you leave, read how to pay Italian fees with an international card from Tunisia first. For the short version inside your first 30 days, see the bank account step of the arrival guide.

1. What to bring before you walk into a bank

Get your codice fiscale first: every Italian bank asks for it. It normally comes with your residence permit application; see how to get your codice fiscale.

Bring the permit receipt

If you have already applied for your residence permit, always show the receipt (ricevuta) with your passport. Banks expect it, and it is the document that shows you are legally staying in Italy while the card is being made.

  • Required in my case: codice fiscale, passport (with your study visa) and proof of enrolment at your university.
  • Useful to bring, in my experience: your pre-enrolment summary and the certificate from your Tunisian bank about your blocked funds, showing the monthly transfers that will arrive in your Italian account (see the blocked account section).
  • Also keep with you: the post office receipt (ricevuta) of your residence permit application.

The extra papers are not official requirements. They simply show the branch who you are, why you are in Rome and where your money comes from.

2. Intesa Sanpaolo: the best first bank, in my experience

Intesa Sanpaolo is one of Italy's largest banks, with branches all over Rome. Its official FAQ↗ (opens in a new tab) says a foreign citizen living in Italy can open an account in the app, online or in a branch, only with an Italian codice fiscale.

The current offer for new customers is XME Conto Silver at EUR 5.90 a month (fee sheet updated 1 October 2026), or EUR 2.90 a month once a salary is credited. The first debit card has no fee, and withdrawals at the group's ATMs are free (XME Conto Silver↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Intesa itself has no under-35 account. The group's app-only bank, isybank↗ (opens in a new tab), offers its isyPrime plan at EUR 0 a month until age 35. I could not confirm isybank's requirements for non-EU students, so check its FAQ before you rely on it.

Step 1: choose a branch near a university

In my experience and that of other students, branches near universities and campuses are used to non-EU students and usually open these accounts without trouble. Ask your university's international office or older students which branch they use.

Step 2: ask for a current account and show your papers

Say you are an international student and want a current account (conto corrente). Show the documents from section 1 and ask which papers they still need, if any.

If one branch says no

In my experience, a refusal at one branch does not mean the bank has refused you. Often that branch simply did not know the procedure for non-EU students. Try another branch, ideally one near a campus. Requirements can change, so check Intesa's official pages.

3. Revolut: an Italian IBAN from the app

Revolut gives an Italian IBAN (starting with IT) to customers who live in Italy (Revolut help). As of October 2026 its plans cost: Standard free, Plus EUR 3.99, Premium EUR 9.99 and Metal EUR 15.99 a month.

Revolut does not open personal accounts for residents of Tunisia (supported countries), so open it after you arrive, with your Italian address. Expect to verify your ID with a selfie, and have your codice fiscale ready in case the app asks for it.

If the app asks for a Tessera Sanitaria

During onboarding, the app may ask for a Tessera Sanitaria (the Italian health card), which most new students do not have yet. In my experience and that of other students, uploading the official codice fiscale document instead worked. It may be rejected at first; resubmitting it usually sends the account to manual review by a person. My review took 3 business days and was approved.

Only your own genuine documents

No official Revolut page I found states the Tessera Sanitaria requirement, so this is experience, not a rule. Only ever submit your own genuine documents.

5. Wise: the easy last option

Wise is not a bank, but its account gives you EUR account details (issued by Wise Europe in Brussels, so the IBAN is Belgian) and a debit card. You need a registered address in a country where Wise offers EUR details (Wise help).

  • Registration and the account are free; the physical card is EUR 7 (Wise card fees).
  • ATM withdrawals are free up to EUR 250 a month, then 2.69% of the extra.
  • Currency conversion starts from 0.2%, depending on the currency (Wise pricing).

You can probably register from Tunisia, but the Wise card is not available there (card countries). I could not confirm that Wise receives transfers in dinars from Tunisia, so do not plan your monthly transfers around it.

6. The conto di base: your legal right to an account

Italian law (TUB, art. 126-noviesdecies) gives every consumer legally staying in the EU the right to a basic account, regardless of where they live. On 5 August 2026, the Bank of Italy↗ (opens in a new tab) told banks that access was sometimes blocked without reason and that their systems must accept legitimate documents such as the receipt of the residence permit application.

Intesa Sanpaolo conto di base (as of October 2026)

At Intesa the conto di base↗ (opens in a new tab) costs EUR 30 a year and is free with an ISEE under EUR 11,600. You must not hold another payment account.

The right is not absolute: anti-money-laundering checks still apply. If a bank refuses, ask for the reason in writing; the Bank of Italy expects banks to explain refusals.

7. Which account should you open first?

  • Main account: Intesa Sanpaolo, at a branch near your campus, as soon as you have your codice fiscale.
  • Need an IBAN today: Revolut from the app, once you have an Italian address.
  • Backup card: Wise.
  • Refused everywhere: ask for a conto di base, with your permit receipt.

Whatever you choose, the IBAN must be in your own name: DiSCo pays the scholarship to your account, see when DiSCo pays.

Frequently asked questions

Can I open an Italian bank account with only a passport and codice fiscale?

In my experience, yes, together with proof of enrolment: that is how I opened my Intesa Sanpaolo account. Officially, Intesa requires an Italian codice fiscale for foreigners living in Italy, and branches may ask for more.

Which bank is best for international students in Rome?

In my experience and that of other students, Intesa Sanpaolo, at a branch near a university. Revolut is a good app alternative once you live in Italy.

Does Revolut give an Italian IBAN?

Yes, to customers resident in Italy. Revolut does not serve residents of Tunisia, so open it after you arrive, with your Italian address.

What if a bank branch refuses to open my account?

Try another branch, ideally near a campus: in my experience the first branch often did not know the procedure. You can also request a conto di base and ask for any refusal in writing.

Can I open a bank account in Italy before I arrive?

Not with Intesa or Revolut in practice: Intesa needs an Italian codice fiscale and Revolut does not accept residents of Tunisia. Wise registration may work from Tunisia, but its card is not available there.
