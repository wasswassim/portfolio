You have just arrived in Rome. Which bank account should you open to get an IBAN for rent, DiSCo and a salary, and what will the bank ask you for?

The short answer: in my experience and that of other students, Intesa Sanpaolo is the best first choice. I opened a normal current account (about EUR 6 a month) with only my codice fiscale, my passport and proof of enrolment. Revolut is the fast alternative in the app, Wise is the easy backup, and the conto di base is a legal right if a bank says no.

This is part 1 of the series on settling in Rome after you arrive. If you still have to pay fees before you leave, read how to pay Italian fees with an international card from Tunisia first. For where the IBAN fits among your other tasks, see the IBAN step in your first 30 days in Rome.

1. What documents do you need to open a bank account in Italy?

To open a bank account in Italy, you need an Italian codice fiscale and your passport. In my case, Intesa Sanpaolo also asked for proof of enrolment. Get the codice fiscale first, because every Italian bank asks for it. The codice fiscale normally comes with your residence permit application. See how to get your codice fiscale in Rome.

Bring the permit receipt

If you have already applied for your residence permit, always show the receipt (ricevuta) with your passport. Banks expect it, and it is the document that shows you are legally staying in Italy while the card is being made.

  • Required in my case: codice fiscale, passport (with your study visa) and proof of enrolment at your university.
  • Useful to bring, in my experience: your pre-enrolment summary and the certificate from your Tunisian bank about your blocked funds, showing the monthly transfers that will arrive in your Italian account (see how the blocked account for the student visa works).
  • Also keep with you: the post office receipt (ricevuta) of your residence permit application.

The extra papers in this list are not official requirements. They simply show the bank branch who you are, why you are in Rome and where your money comes from.

2. Is Intesa Sanpaolo the best first bank for a student?

In my experience and that of other students, yes: Intesa Sanpaolo is the best first bank for a non-EU student in Rome, and I opened my current account there with only my codice fiscale, passport and proof of enrolment. Intesa Sanpaolo is one of Italy's largest banks, with branches all over Rome. Its official FAQ↗ (opens in a new tab) says a foreign citizen living in Italy can open an account in the app, online or in a branch, only with an Italian codice fiscale.

Intesa Sanpaolo's current offer for new customers is XME Conto Silver at EUR 5.90 a month (fee sheet updated 1 October 2026), or EUR 2.90 a month once a salary is credited. The first debit card has no fee, and withdrawals at the group's ATMs are free (XME Conto Silver↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Intesa itself has no under-35 account. The group's app-only bank, isybank↗ (opens in a new tab), offers its isyPrime plan at EUR 0 a month until age 35. I could not confirm isybank's requirements for non-EU students, so check its FAQ before you rely on it.

Step 1: choose a branch near a university

In my experience and that of other students, Intesa Sanpaolo branches near universities and campuses are used to non-EU students and usually open these accounts without trouble. Ask your university's international office or older students which branch they use.

Step 2: ask for a current account and show your papers

Say you are an international student and want a current account (conto corrente). Show your codice fiscale, passport and proof of enrolment, and ask the Intesa Sanpaolo branch which papers it still needs, if any.

If one branch says no

In my experience, a refusal at one Intesa Sanpaolo branch does not mean the bank has refused you. Often that branch simply did not know the procedure for non-EU students. Try another branch, ideally one near a campus. Requirements can change, so check Intesa's official pages.

3. Can a student get an Italian IBAN with Revolut?

Yes: Revolut↗ (opens in a new tab) gives an Italian IBAN (starting with IT) to customers who live in Italy (Revolut help), so a student can get one in the app after arriving. As of October 2026, Revolut's plans cost: Standard free, Plus EUR 3.99, Premium EUR 9.99 and Metal EUR 15.99 a month.

Revolut does not open personal accounts for residents of Tunisia (supported countries), so open it after you arrive, with your Italian address. Expect to verify your ID with a selfie, and have your codice fiscale ready in case the app asks for it.

If the app asks for a Tessera Sanitaria

During onboarding, the Revolut app may ask for a Tessera Sanitaria (the Italian health card), which most new students do not have yet. In my experience and that of other students, uploading the official codice fiscale document instead worked. The codice fiscale document may be rejected at first. Resubmitting it usually sends the account to manual review by a person. My review took 3 business days and was approved.

Once approved, the Revolut account gives you an Italian IBAN straight in the app. If you want one, you can sign up through the partner link below.

5. Is Wise a good backup account in Italy?

Yes, Wise is an easy backup, and the last option to try: Wise↗ (opens in a new tab) is not a bank, but the Wise account gives you EUR account details (issued by Wise Europe in Brussels, so the IBAN is Belgian) and a debit card. You need a registered address in a country where Wise offers EUR details (Wise help).

6. What is the conto di base, and can a bank refuse it?

The conto di base is a basic bank account that Italian law (TUB, art. 126-noviesdecies) gives to every consumer legally staying in the EU, regardless of where they live. On 5 August 2026, the Bank of Italy↗ (opens in a new tab) told banks that access was sometimes blocked without reason and that their systems must accept legitimate documents such as the receipt of the residence permit application.

At Intesa the conto di base↗ (opens in a new tab) costs EUR 30 a year and is free with an ISEE under EUR 11,600. To get the Intesa Sanpaolo conto di base, you must not hold another payment account.

The right to a conto di base is not absolute: anti-money-laundering checks still apply. If a bank refuses, ask for the reason in writing. The Bank of Italy expects banks to explain refusals.

7. Which account should you open first?

In my experience, most students in Rome should open a current account at Intesa Sanpaolo first. Revolut, Wise and the conto di base cover the other cases.

  • Main account: Intesa Sanpaolo, at a branch near your campus, as soon as you have your codice fiscale.
  • Need an IBAN today: Revolut from the app, once you have an Italian address.
  • Backup card: Wise.
  • Refused everywhere: ask for a conto di base, with your permit receipt.

Whatever account you choose, the IBAN must be in your own name, because DiSCo Lazio pays the scholarship to your account. See when DiSCo Lazio pays the scholarship.

With an Italian IBAN in your name, the next settling steps are to register your residenza in Rome at the anagrafe and to find a student job in Rome, where employers pay the salary by bank transfer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I open an Italian bank account with only a passport and codice fiscale?

Yes, in my experience, as long as you also show proof of enrolment: that is how I opened my Intesa Sanpaolo current account in Rome. Officially, Intesa Sanpaolo requires an Italian codice fiscale from foreign citizens living in Italy, and a branch may still ask for more papers, such as the receipt (ricevuta) of your residence permit application.

Which bank is best for international students in Rome?

In my experience and that of other students, Intesa Sanpaolo is the best first bank, at a branch near a university where staff know the procedure for non-EU students. Intesa Sanpaolo's XME Conto Silver costs EUR 5.90 a month as of October 2026. Revolut is a good app alternative once you live in Italy, because Revolut gives Italy residents an Italian IBAN.

Does Revolut give an Italian IBAN?

Yes, Revolut gives an Italian IBAN, starting with IT, to customers resident in Italy. Revolut does not open personal accounts for residents of Tunisia, so a Tunisian student should sign up after arriving, with an Italian address. In my experience, if the Revolut app asks for a Tessera Sanitaria, the official codice fiscale document can work instead after a manual review.

Can I get a Wise card in Tunisia?

No, not as of October 2026: you can probably register with Wise from Tunisia, but the Wise card is not available to residents of Tunisia, which is not one of Wise's card countries. Once you live in Italy, you can order the Wise card as an Italy resident for EUR 7. Do not plan your monthly transfers in dinars around Wise, because receiving dinar transfers from a Tunisian bank could not be confirmed.

What if a bank branch refuses to open my account?

Try another branch first, ideally one near a campus: in my experience, the first branch often did not know the procedure for non-EU students. If banks keep refusing, ask for a conto di base, the basic account Italian law gives anyone legally staying in the EU, and ask for the reason for any refusal in writing, as the Bank of Italy expects banks to explain refusals.

Can I open a bank account in Italy before I arrive?

In practice, no, not with Intesa Sanpaolo or Revolut: Intesa Sanpaolo needs an Italian codice fiscale from a foreign citizen living in Italy, and Revolut does not accept residents of Tunisia. Wise registration may work from Tunisia, but the Wise card is not available there, so plan to open your main account in your first weeks in Rome.
