Important notice

This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Visa rules, amounts and fees can change from one year to the next, so always check the Italian Embassy in Tunis and your bank before you pay or submit anything.

You have been admitted to an Italian university. How do you get the student visa from Tunisia?

Here is the short answer. After your university validates your Universitaly pre-enrolment, Almaviva (the Embassy's visa centre) emails you an appointment. You bring 15 documents, including proof that EUR 10,179.85 is blocked for you at a bank, pay the fees in cash, and wait up to 90 days. For 2027/28, Bachelor's and Master's students must apply by 31 October 2027.

This is part 5 of our guide to studying in Italy for Tunisian students. The earlier parts cover choosing a university and the Universitaly pre-enrolment, translating and apostilling your documents and the DiSCo scholarship.

1. Which visa do Tunisian students need for Italy?

You need a long-stay Visa D “Immatricolazione Università” (study, university enrolment). It is valid for 365 days. If your university validated your pre-enrolment “con riserva” (with reservations, for example while a document is still being checked), the visa is valid for 100 days instead.

The visa is tied to the course on your Universitaly pre-enrolment. If you change course, you need a new visa application from Tunisia.

2. How do you get the Almaviva appointment?

You do not book it. The Embassy's notice of 1 June 2026↗ (opens in a new tab) explains the order:

  1. Your university checks your file and validates your pre-enrolment on Universitaly↗ (opens in a new tab). The file then reaches the Italian Embassy in Tunis.
  2. Almaviva emails you at the address you used on Universitaly, with your appointment date. Check that inbox and your spam folder every day.
  3. The date cannot be changed, so prepare your documents as soon as you are admitted, not after the email arrives.
  4. On the day, go to the Almaviva centre with the complete file. Your fingerprints are taken there.

Almaviva (AVS) is at 8 Rue Alphonse Laveran, 1002 Tunis, open Monday to Friday 09:00-17:00 and Saturday 09:00-13:00 (phone +216 70103710 or +216 31303041), as listed on the Embassy's where to apply page↗ (opens in a new tab). The Embassy itself gives no appointments and accepts no visa applications by email.

Use one email address

Write your Universitaly email somewhere safe and do not change it. If you lose access to it, you may miss the only appointment you get.

3. Which documents does the student visa require?

The Embassy's info sheet (July 2026) lists 15 items. Read it yourself in Italian↗ (opens in a new tab) or in English and French↗ (opens in a new tab), because it is the reference Almaviva uses.

  1. The visa application form, signed.
  2. Your passport, valid for more than 15 months.
  3. A recent ICAO-standard photo.
  4. The Universitaly pre-enrolment summary, in two copies.
  5. Your original secondary-school diploma (bac) and a copy, translated, apostilled and with the DOV or CIMEA statement.
  6. A motivation letter (optional).
  7. The aptitude test result, if your course requires one.
  8. A language certificate: Italian B2 or higher, or English for an English-taught course.
  9. A return ticket booking.
  10. Proof of accommodation for your first period in Italy.
  11. Health insurance.
  12. Proof of financial means and of who covers them (payslips, 6 months of bank statements, CNSS certificate...).
  13. The bank's original “dichiarazione di blocage”, signed and stamped by the branch manager, with your RIB.
  14. Your birth extract and family record, apostilled and translated.
  15. A statement of your socio-economic situation from the competent Tunisian ministry.

Documents in French or Arabic must be translated into Italian and apostilled: part 3 explains how to legalise and apostille them, who can translate them and whether you need the DOV or CIMEA. Check your passport date first: if it expires within 15 months, renew it before anything else.

4. How does the blocked account work with a Tunisian bank?

For 2026/27, the Embassy asks for EUR 10,179.85 per year, that is EUR 783.06 for 13 months. The amount must be blocked irrevocably for a year and released at EUR 783.06 per month. The 2027/28 figure may be updated, so check the new notice. In Tunisia, the transfers abroad are governed by BCT circular 2025-10↗ (opens in a new tab) of 5 September 2025.

Step 1: Open your student file at one bank

Choose one Tunisian bank for the academic year and open your student file there. Bring your CIN, your bac, the boursier or non-boursier attestation from the Ministry of Higher Education, your registration certificate (the pre-registration certificate is accepted at first, if the registration certificate follows within 3 months) and a signed commitment to repatriate the funds.

Step 2: Block the full amount and get the attestation

Block the full amount at the bank in Tunisia. The bank then gives you the original dichiarazione di blocage (attestation de blocage), signed and stamped by the branch manager, with your RIB. This original goes in your visa file.

Step 3: After you enrol in Italy, send the papers back

Once you have finalised your enrolment in Rome, send your enrolment papers to the bank in Tunisia. The bank can then let your parents transfer money to you each month, within the BCT ceilings below.

The installation allowance is what most students use for their first weeks: see the first-month budget in Rome.

If the visa is refused

Under article 26 of the BCT circular, funds transferred for a student who does not get the visa must be recovered. Ask your bank how this works before you sign.

A scholarship can reduce the amount you must block: an Italian-government scholarship of at least the same amount replaces it, and a regional grant such as DiSCo counts only once it is awarded (see when DiSCo pays).

5. Health insurance and proof of accommodation

Your insurance must cover medical and hospital care, with no limit on urgent hospitalisation. The Embassy sets no fixed minimum in euros and accepts three forms:

  • A consular declaration under the Italy-Tunisia agreement.
  • A foreign policy with a consular declaration that it is valid in Italy.
  • A policy from an Italian insurer.

For accommodation, a hotel booking or a hospitality declaration from someone in Italy works. Applying for a university residence is not valid proof, even if you applied to DiSCo.

6. How much does the Italian student visa cost?

Check the Embassy's fees page↗ (opens in a new tab) a few days before your appointment, as the dinar amounts follow the exchange rate.

7. Processing time, interview and deadline

The Embassy can take up to 90 days to decide, and it may call you for an interview. For 2027/28, the MUR circular↗ (opens in a new tab) sets the visa deadline for Bachelor's and Master's courses at 31 October 2027. PhD students have no fixed date but must apply before teaching starts. Since each university can only validate a limited number of foreign students, apply early.

Student visa: frequently asked questions

How do I get an appointment for an Italian student visa in Tunisia?

You do not book it. After your university validates your Universitaly pre-enrolment, Almaviva emails you a date at its Tunis centre, at the address you used on Universitaly. The date cannot be changed.

How much is the blocked amount for an Italian student visa?

EUR 10,179.85 for 2026/27 (EUR 783.06 x 13 months), blocked irrevocably for a year. Check the 2027/28 notice for any update.

How long does the Italian student visa take?

Up to 90 days after your appointment, according to the Embassy. You may be called for an interview.

Can I change university after getting the visa?

Not on the same visa. The visa is linked to the course on your pre-enrolment; changing course means a new visa application from Tunisia.

How much does the Italian student visa cost in dinars?

170.600 TND for the visa (EUR 50) plus 68.300 TND for the Almaviva service, paid in cash at the centre. The amounts can change.

Can I send my visa application to the Embassy by email?

No. The Embassy accepts no applications by email; you submit the file in person at Almaviva on your appointment date.
