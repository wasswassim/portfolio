Can you use RedotPay in Tunisia, and is it a safe way to pay in euros?

The short answer: RedotPay's official pages do not exclude Tunisian citizens or users located in Tunisia (checked 10 October 2026). But Tunisian exchange law says foreign-currency operations go through authorised intermediaries, and no Tunisian text authorises residents to buy or hold crypto. For a resident of Tunisia, funding a crypto card is legally risky, and we do not recommend it.

The practical parts below (P2P, wallet safety) are written for readers who already live abroad, for example students in Italy, where they can use such services lawfully. For the legal ways to pay from Tunisia, start with our guide to paying Italian fees with an international card.

1. What is RedotPay and how does the card work?

RedotPay describes itself as "a fintech service provider and not a bank" (RedotPay↗ (opens in a new tab)). You deposit crypto, mainly stablecoins such as USDT or USDC, and spend it with a Visa card (the network named in its Terms↗ (opens in a new tab)). The physical card also works at ATMs.

RedotPay card fees (as of October 2026)

There is no monthly or annual maintenance fee. Funding with a bank card costs 3% (minimum 1 USD).

  • Limits: up to 100,000 USD per transaction; ATM withdrawals 3 a day and 30 a month, up to 3,750 USD a day on a USD card (lower in some countries) (fee and limit table↗ (opens in a new tab)).
  • KYC: a photo of your original ID, a face scan, your home address and 4-5 questions on your job and source of funds (identity verification↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Risks to know before you deposit

The Terms allow RedotPay to freeze or close an account "at any time, without notice". Users on Trustpilot↗ (opens in a new tab) report funds blocked for weeks during source-of-funds checks. In August 2026, Binance affiliates sued RedotPay's co-founders in Hong Kong for about USD 472.8 million; these are allegations, no ruling has been reported, and RedotPay says it is defending itself.

2. Is a crypto card legal for residents of Tunisia?

No Tunisian law or Central Bank (BCT) circular that expressly authorises buying crypto or stablecoins could be found. The exchange code (law 76-18↗ (opens in a new tab)) sets the frame:

  • Art. 3: foreign-exchange operations go only through the BCT or authorised intermediaries (banks, La Poste).
  • Art. 16 and 17: residents must declare their assets abroad and may not dispose of them without authorisation. A crypto balance on a foreign platform is arguably such an asset.
  • Art. 21: settlements between residents take place in Tunisia and in dinars.
  • Art. 35: offences are punishable by 1 month to 5 years in prison and a fine of at least five times the amount involved.

Buying USDT with dinars, through P2P or a foreign card, sits outside these channels. A new exchange code (bill 115/2025) is under discussion but had not been adopted at the last report found. Read more in the exchange rules section of our payment guide.

4. Why this guide does not recommend Rizon

Rizon is a stablecoin app with a Visa card, but it decides eligibility by passport. Its help centre (July 2026) does not list Tunisia among supported nationalities, its Terms say the product is intended for residents of the United States and Latin America, and its help centre says a KYC rejection is final. A Tunisian passport holder would very likely be refused, so we do not link to it.

5. P2P explained: how an escrow order works

P2P (peer-to-peer) means buying crypto directly from another person on a platform. You pay them in local money, and the platform holds their crypto in escrow until they confirm your payment. Binance P2P and RedotPay's P2P Marketplace both work this way. Binance's Terms↗ (opens in a new tab) exclude users for whom using it would be illegal locally, even if their country is not on its prohibited list↗ (opens in a new tab).

How escrow protects (and does not protect) a P2P order, for readers living abroad

  1. Choose an adIn the P2P zone you pick a seller; in Express the system picks one. Read the seller's terms and check their completion rate.
  2. The crypto is lockedWhen you place the order, the seller's crypto is reserved in Binance escrow.
  3. Pay outside the appPay with the method shown in the order, from an account in your own name, within the payment window (often 15 to 45 minutes).
  4. Notify, then waitTap "Transferred, notify seller" only after paying. The seller checks and releases the crypto. Never cancel after paying; use the Appeal button instead (Binance guide↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Fees and the name rule

On Binance's P2P fee page↗ (opens in a new tab) (checked 10 October 2026), a taker in the P2P zone paid 0%, a maker 0.20%, and Express cost the taker 0.10%. The real cost is the gap between buy and sell prices, plus bank fees. The payer's name must match the counterparty's verified name. RedotPay says its P2P "does not guarantee the settlement" of trades (P2P Marketplace↗ (opens in a new tab)). Buying crypto with dinars from Tunisia is exposed under the exchange code (see section 2); this guide does not explain how to do it.

6. Sending stablecoins safely, step by step

For readers abroad. Crypto sends cannot be reversed. In the EU, Binance notes MiCA restrictions on unauthorised stablecoins, so USDC may be easier than USDT.

Step 1: same coin and same network on both sides

Choose the coin and network in the receiving app first, then exactly the same pair in the sending app (RedotPay withdrawals↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Stop and check

USDT on Tron (TRC20) is not USDT on BNB Smart Chain. A network the receiver does not list can mean lost funds. An unexpected memo field means: stop.

Step 2: copy the address with the copy button or QR code

Use the app's copy button or scan its QR code. Never type an address by hand.

Stop and check

Clipboard malware can swap a copied address. Install apps only from official stores.

Step 3: compare the first and last characters

Compare the first 6 and last 6 characters with the original, plus a few in the middle.

Stop and check

If one character differs, cancel and copy again.

Step 4: never copy an address from your history

In address poisoning, scammers send a tiny transfer from a lookalike address so it shows in your history. Copy only from the receiving app or your address book.

Stop and check

An unexpected tiny deposit is a warning sign. Never reuse its address.

Step 5: send a small test first

Send a small amount above the minimum, wait for it, then send the rest to the same saved address.

Stop and check

The test only protects you if the main transfer goes to exactly the same address.

Step 6: check the TxID on a block explorer

Paste the TxID into the network's explorer (Solscan, BscScan, PolygonScan) to see the amount, receiver and status (Binance Academy↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Stop and check

Pending is not lost. Do not send again before it is confirmed.

Step 7: whitelist your addresses

Turn on the withdrawal whitelist↗ (opens in a new tab) and save each address with a clear label, coin and network.

Stop and check

Changing the whitelist can pause withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours. Plan ahead of a deadline.

Step 8: never share your seed phrase or 2FA codes

Real support staff never ask for your seed phrase, passwords or 2FA codes.

Stop and check

Anyone who asks for them, by chat, email or phone, is a scammer.

7. Glossary of crypto terms

Key facts

Wallet
An app or account that holds your crypto and lets you send and receive it.
Address
A long string of letters and numbers, like an IBAN for crypto.
Network / chain
The road the money travels on (Solana, BNB Smart Chain, Tron…). Both sides must use the same one.
Stablecoin
A token designed to stay worth 1 US dollar.
USDT
The most common dollar stablecoin, issued by Tether.
USDC
A dollar stablecoin issued by Circle.
Gas fee
The fee paid to process a transfer on a network.
P2P
Buying crypto directly from another person on a platform.
Escrow
The platform locks the seller's crypto until the deal is complete.
KYC
Identity verification (ID and face scan) before you can use the service.
Test transaction
A small send to check the address before the main transfer.
Block explorer
A public website where anyone can look up a transfer.
TxID
The unique reference of a transfer, like a bank transfer reference.

Frequently asked questions

Does RedotPay work in Tunisia?

Technically yes: as of October 2026, Tunisia is not on RedotPay's unsupported-nationality or blocked-location lists. Legally, no Tunisian text authorises residents to fund a crypto card, so it is risky and we do not recommend it from Tunisia.

How much does the RedotPay card cost?

10 USD for the virtual card and 100 USD for the physical card, with no monthly fee. Payments in another currency add 1.2%, and the crypto conversion adds 1%.

Can a Tunisian open a Rizon account?

Very likely not: Tunisia is not on Rizon's list of supported nationalities, and Rizon says a KYC rejection is final.

What is Binance P2P and is it safe?

It is a marketplace where you buy crypto from another user while Binance holds the seller's crypto in escrow. Escrow reduces the risk but does not stop fake payment proofs, third-party payments or off-platform scams.

What happens if I send USDT on the wrong network?

The funds are usually lost. Binance's Terms say crypto sent to an incompatible address is lost, and any recovery attempt is paid and not guaranteed.

Can RedotPay freeze my account?

Yes. Its Terms allow it to freeze or close an account at any time without notice, and users report blocks during source-of-funds checks.
