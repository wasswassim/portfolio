This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Immigration programs, requirements and procedures can change, so always check the official sources before applying or paying anything.

You have completed an approved training program in Tunisia. How do you get from there to a work contract in Italy?

This is part 2 of our guide to working in Italy from Tunisia. Part 1 explains the training-abroad route, who qualifies, the documents and the training itself. This part covers what comes next: the Italian employer, the work contract, the Nulla Osta, the work visa and your first steps in Italy.

In short: an Italian employer matched with you through an approved program offers the work contract and files the Nulla Osta request. Once the Nulla Osta is issued, you apply for a National Visa D from Tunisia. After you arrive, you ask the Sportello Unico for an appointment within 8 working days to sign the Contratto di soggiorno, and the Permesso di soggiorno procedure follows.

1. What happens after the training is completed?

After finishing the qualifying program, the hiring stage and the procedures for entering Italy begin.

The Italian employer is the one who files the Nulla Osta request on the worker's behalf. The worker does not file a Nulla Osta request independently.

For workers who met the conditions of the training-abroad route, entry procedures follow the system specific to this route, outside the Decreto Flussi quotas.

2. How do you get a work contract in Italy from Tunisia?

A legitimate work contract is not something you buy or receive from an intermediary. On this route, it comes from an Italian employer who has been matched with you through an approved program, and who then files the Nulla Osta request.

Do not confuse two different documents. The work contract (contratto di lavoro) is the agreement between you and the employer. The Contratto di soggiorno is the residence contract you complete after arriving in Italy, as explained in section 7.

Before you accept an offer, check that:

  • The employer's name and details are given, and you can verify who they are.
  • The job, the sector and the program match what you trained for.
  • The request goes through an official channel, not a private message.
  • Nobody asks you to pay for a contract or a guaranteed visa.

3. What is the Nulla Osta?

Nulla Osta al lavoro is the authorization required to complete the process of a foreign worker entering Italy to work.

In simple terms: the worker is qualified → there is an Italian employer → the employer files the Nulla Osta request → after approval, the visa stage begins.

Your employer files this request online, on the Italian Ministry of the Interior's Portale Servizi ALI↗ (opens in a new tab), with the form for workers trained abroad. The portal checks that your name is on the list of workers who completed an approved program, so a Nulla Osta request cannot be filed for someone who is not on it. If your employer needs help, Portale Integrazione Migranti has a step-by-step guide for employers↗ (opens in a new tab).

Warning

Do not pay anyone who sells you a “ready-made Nulla Osta”. You must know who the employer is and which official channel the request is filed through.

4. What is SIISL?

There is an important update in 2026 that job seekers should not miss. SIISL is the Sistema Informativo per l'Inclusione Sociale e Lavorativa, an Italian system that aims to support access to inclusion and job opportunities.

Procedures have been set for registering, in this system, foreign citizens who completed training programs in their home country under this route. This is a recent addition, so you may find older guides online that do not mention SIISL.

Under the new rules, workers who completed an approved program are registered automatically, so you do not need to sign up yourself. The platform is at siisl.lavoro.gov.it↗ (opens in a new tab).

5. How do you apply for the work visa from Tunisia?

Once the Nulla Osta is issued, the visa application stage begins. For residents of Tunisia, rely on the information published by the Italian Embassy in Tunisia and the authorized body that processes visa applications.

For a long stay for work, the visa is a National Visa D, depending on the nature of the case.

Visa information: Italian Embassy in Tunisia: visas↗ (opens in a new tab)

Where to apply for a visa: Where to apply for a visa↗ (opens in a new tab)

Visa applications from Tunisia go through the embassy's service provider, ALMAVIVA (avs.com.tn↗ (opens in a new tab)), which handles appointments. The embassy itself does not give appointments or accept visa applications by email.

6. When must the visa application be filed?

For workers who completed training programs abroad, a deadline counted from the end of training applies to the visa application. Official sources give different figures depending on the date and the step (earlier Ministry guidance stated 6 months for the visa, while the SIISL rules mention 12 months for requesting the Nulla Osta), so check the Ministry's current FAQ for the exact deadline in your case before you plan anything.

7. What happens after arriving in Italy?

Arriving in Italy does not mean the procedures are over. You must complete the procedures for the Contratto di soggiorno and then the Permesso di soggiorno, according to the procedures that apply to your case.

According to the official guide on Portale Integrazione Migranti↗ (opens in a new tab), within 8 working days of entering Italy you must ask the Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione for an appointment to sign the Contratto di soggiorno. When you sign it, the Sportello Unico also issues your tax code certificate and has you sign the Permesso di soggiorno application.

Confirm this deadline yourself

Some recent sources say this deadline became 15 days after a 2025 change in the law, and booking procedures differ from one Prefettura to another. Before you travel, confirm the current deadline with the Sportello Unico of the province where you will work, or with your employer.

Always keep copies of applications, receipts, appointments, correspondence and official documents.

8. Checklist: from work contract to residence permit

Before you travel, and in your first weeks in Italy, check each step:

  • You know who your employer is, and the job matches your approved training.
  • Your employer filed the Nulla Osta request through the official channel, and it has been issued.
  • You applied for the National Visa D from Tunisia within the deadline that applies to your case.
  • You know you must ask the Sportello Unico for an appointment within 8 working days of entering Italy to sign the Contratto di soggiorno, and you have confirmed the current deadline.
  • You then complete the Permesso di soggiorno procedure.
  • You keep copies of every application, receipt, appointment and official document.

If someone offered you any of these steps in exchange for money, read how to recognise job scams before you go further.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an employer to work in Italy on this route?

Yes. At the Nulla Osta stage, there must be an Italian employer who files the nominative request.

What is the difference between a work contract and the Contratto di soggiorno?

The work contract is the agreement between you and the employer. The Contratto di soggiorno is the residence contract you complete after arriving in Italy.

Can I work in Italy without a contract?

Not legally on this route. An Italian employer must file the Nulla Osta request first. Be wary of any offer of work in Italy that skips these steps.

Which visa do I need to work in Italy?

For a long stay for work, a National Visa D, requested from Tunisia through the Italian Embassy and its authorized body once the Nulla Osta is issued.

When must I apply for the work visa?

Within a deadline counted from the end of your training. Official sources give different figures, so check the Italian Ministry of Labour's current FAQ for your case.

What do I do after arriving in Italy?

Within 8 working days of entering Italy, you ask the Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione for an appointment to sign the Contratto di soggiorno, then you complete the Permesso di soggiorno procedure. Confirm the current deadline with the Sportello Unico, as the rules can change.
