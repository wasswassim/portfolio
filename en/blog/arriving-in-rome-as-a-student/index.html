You have landed in Rome with your study visa. What do you have to do, and in what order, during your first month?

The short answer: within 8 working days of arrival, request your residence permit for study (permesso di soggiorno per studio) at a post office. Your codice fiscale comes with that application, and the post office receipt lets you open an account, finalise enrolment and complete your DiSCo steps.

This is part 6 of our guide to studying in Italy for Tunisian students, after the visa: your first 30 days in Rome, step by step.

1. Your first 30 days in Rome, in order

  1. Within 8 working days: send the residence permit kit from a post office.
  2. Only if a landlord or bank needs it sooner: get your codice fiscale at the Agenzia delle Entrate.
  3. Open a Postepay Evolution card or a bank account.
  4. Finalise enrolment at your university.
  5. Send the enrolment papers to your bank in Tunisia.
  6. DiSCo: upload the permit receipt, book a partner CAF, sign the ISEEUP, add your IBAN and PEC.
  7. Choose your health cover, then look for student work.

2. How do you request the residence permit for study?

You must apply within 8 working days of entering Italy (Polizia di Stato↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Step 1: get the yellow-band kit

Go to a post office with a Sportello Amico desk and ask for the residence permit kit, the envelope with a yellow band. Poste Italiane↗ (opens in a new tab) explains how to fill it in. Write your name exactly as in your passport.

Step 2: put the right documents in the envelope

  • The completed application form from the kit.
  • A copy of your passport, including the page with your visa.
  • A copy of your health insurance, valid for the whole length of the permit, and proof of your means (your international office can confirm the full list).
  • A EUR 16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo), bought at a tobacconist.

Step 3: pay and keep the receipt

The clerk gives you a receipt with a username and password. Keep it with your passport: your university, the bank and DiSCo will ask for it.

Step 4: go to the Questura for fingerprints

You also receive a convocation letter with your appointment at the Questura, where your fingerprints are taken. Check the status of your permit on the Polizia di Stato permit portal↗ (opens in a new tab) with your receipt details. The Polizia gives about 60 days for processing; Sapienza warns it can take about 90.

Do not leave Italy before you have the permit

The Italian Embassy in Tunis advises students not to leave Italy until the residence permit is issued.

Sapienza students can get help on campus: its residence permit page↗ (opens in a new tab) lists the campus police desk and the Questura immigration office (Via Teofilo Patini 23).

3. How do you get your codice fiscale?

The codice fiscale is your Italian tax code, needed for a lease, a bank or a phone contract. Usually you do not request it: the Questura assigns it with your permit application.

If you need it earlier, go to any Agenzia delle Entrate office with form AA4/8 and your passport with the visa. The Agenzia delle Entrate leaflet for foreign citizens↗ (opens in a new tab) explains the procedure. At Sapienza, the HELLO desk can help you with the forms.

4. Opening a student bank account in Italy

You need an IBAN for DiSCo, rent and a salary. Two common options:

  • Postepay Evolution: a prepaid card with an IBAN, EUR 5 to issue plus EUR 19 a year (Poste Italiane↗ (opens in a new tab)).
  • A bank account: banks ask for your codice fiscale; compare monthly fees.

According to the Banca d'Italia↗ (opens in a new tab), anyone legally staying in the EU has the right to a basic account (conto di base), and a bank may not refuse your residence permit receipt as an identity document.

5. Finalise enrolment and your scholarship file

Enrolment is complete only once your university has checked your originals:

  • Sapienza: send your permit receipt to the International Student Office↗ (opens in a new tab), then book the check of your original documents.
  • Tor Vergata: show your visa, originals and permit (or receipt) at the International Students Office, then pay the first instalment (documents required↗ (opens in a new tab)).
  • Roma Tre: book an appointment at the foreign-qualifications office, Via Ostiense 129, and bring your originals (non-EU enrolment↗ (opens in a new tab)).

Unlock the monthly transfers from Tunisia

Once you are enrolled, send the enrolment papers to your bank in Tunisia. The bank can then let your parents transfer up to 4,000 TND a month (about EUR 1,188 at the BCT rate of 7 October 2026), as explained in the blocked account section.

Complete your DiSCo steps

  • Upload your residence permit or the post office receipt in your DiSCo Profile by about 1 December if you requested housing, otherwise by about 10 February (check the 2027/28 call).
  • Book an appointment at a DiSCo partner CAF↗ (opens in a new tab) and sign your ISEEUP by about 10 December (check the 2027/28 call).
  • Add your IBAN and a PEC (certified email) address in your Profile.

If the ISEEUP is done correctly, the first payments follow; see when DiSCo pays the scholarship.

Choose your health cover

Register voluntarily with the national health service (SSN) for about EUR 700 a year (Tor Vergata, 2024↗ (opens in a new tab)), or keep private insurance. Check the current amount with your ASL or university.

6. Budget for your first month in Rome

Your bank in Tunisia can send an installation allowance of up to 6,000 TND per academic year (about EUR 1,782 at the BCT rate of 7 October 2026). The monthly transfers only start after your enrolment papers reach the bank, so this money must last until then.

Buy second-hand on Subito↗ (opens in a new tab): furniture, a bike, books. And never pay a deposit before you have seen the room; read how to avoid housing scams.

7. Can you work while you study in Italy?

Yes, within limits: a student permit allows up to 20 hours a week, for a maximum of 1,040 hours a year (DPR 394/1999, art. 14↗ (opens in a new tab)). If you only have the first-permit receipt, check with the Questura or your university before you start a job.

  • University career offices: Sapienza↗ (opens in a new tab), Tor Vergata↗ (opens in a new tab), Roma Tre↗ (opens in a new tab).
  • Regione Lazio: job offers↗ (opens in a new tab) and SpazioLavoro↗ (opens in a new tab).
  • Job sites: Subito job ads↗ (opens in a new tab), InfoJobs↗ (opens in a new tab), Indeed↗ (opens in a new tab).

Avoid undeclared work

Undeclared work (lavoro nero) leaves you without insurance or legal protection. Read the Ministry of Labour page on labour exploitation↗ (opens in a new tab) and ask for a written contract.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I have to apply for the permesso di soggiorno?

8 working days from your arrival in Italy. You apply by sending the yellow-band kit from a post office with a Sportello Amico desk.

How much does the residence permit for study cost?

About EUR 116.46 at the post office: a EUR 16 revenue stamp, a EUR 30 postal fee and a EUR 70.46 bollettino (amounts checked October 2026).

How do I get a codice fiscale in Rome?

It is normally assigned with your residence permit application. If you need it earlier, go to any Agenzia delle Entrate office with form AA4/8 and your passport with the visa.

Can I open a bank account with the permit receipt?

Yes. According to the Banca d'Italia, a bank may not refuse the residence permit receipt as an identity document, and anyone legally staying in the EU has the right to a basic account.

Can I travel back to Tunisia while I wait for my permit?

The Italian Embassy in Tunis advises against leaving Italy before your residence permit is issued. Plan trips after you collect it.

Can international students work in Italy?

Yes, up to 20 hours a week and 1,040 hours a year. If you only have the first-permit receipt, check with the Questura or your university before you start.
