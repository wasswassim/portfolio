Important notice

This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Admission rules, deadlines, fees and visa procedures can change, so always check the official sources before applying or paying anything.

You are Tunisian and want to study at a university in Rome. Where do you start, and when?

The short answer: you apply to the university first, on its own portal. Once you are admitted, you complete the pre-enrolment on Universitaly, the university validates it, and the Italian Embassy's visa centre contacts you with an appointment. For the 2027/28 academic year, the last date to request the study visa for a Bachelor or Master is 31 October 2027.

That sounds far away, but documents take months and some windows open this autumn. This page gives you the whole path, the calendar and the costs, with a detailed guide for each stage.

1. Who this study guide is for

This series is for Tunisian students who hold the bac or a university degree and want to study in person in Rome or Lazio, for a Bachelor (Laurea), a Master (Laurea Magistrale) or a PhD (Dottorato).

  • Public universities: Sapienza, Tor Vergata and Roma Tre.
  • Private universities: LUISS and LUMSA.
  • Not covered for now: Medicine. Italy changed its access rules in 2025, and the rules for international students have not been published yet.

2. The path to studying in Rome in 7 stages

Every student follows the same order. Each stage has its own guide:

  1. Plan and calendar. This page: choose your target year (2027/28), your courses and your deadlines.
  2. Choose a university and apply. You apply on the university's own portal, then, after admission, complete the Universitaly pre-enrolment. See how to choose a university in Rome and apply.
  3. Prepare and translate your documents. Diplomas are authenticated by the Tunisian ministry, copied, apostilled and translated into Italian. See how to legalise and translate your documents for Italy.
  4. Apply for the DiSCo scholarship. The Lazio regional grant is based on your family's income and also offers residence places. See the DiSCo Lazio scholarship guide.
  5. Get the visa and block the money. The visa centre emails you a date, and you must show a blocked amount. See the Italy student visa and blocked account.
  6. Arrive in Rome. Within 8 working days you request your residence permit, then finish enrolling at your university. See your first weeks in Rome.
  7. Find accommodation. DiSCo residences, university housing services and private rooms. See student housing in Rome.

3. Why the 2027/28 intake means starting now

All the 2026/27 deadlines are over, so the year to aim for is 2027/28. Italy's Ministry of University (MUR) has already published the rules for that year: one circular covers both 2026/27 and 2027/28.

  • Documents are slow. The whole chain, up to the Dichiarazione di Valore or CIMEA statement, can take several months.
  • Some windows are already open. LUISS applications for 2027/28 run from October 2026 to 10 February 2027.
  • Sapienza opens early. Its 2026/27 window ran from 22 December 2025 to 15 May 2026, so the 2027/28 one is expected to open around December 2026 (check the 2027/28 call).
  • Places are capped. From 2026/27, each university can validate only a limited number of foreign pre-enrolments; Universitaly blocks the rest.

5. How much studying in Rome costs

Sapienza and Tor Vergata figures are flat fees for students from Tunisia (Tor Vergata: unless you have an ISEE parificato). Check with your university.

6. How your money reaches you in Italy

Money moves in three steps under the Central Bank of Tunisia's circular 2025-10, through one Tunisian bank per academic year.

  • The blocked amount. The full EUR 10,179.85 is blocked at your bank in Tunisia for the visa. How the blocked account works.
  • The installation allowance. Up to 6,000 TND per academic year (about EUR 1,782 at the BCT rate of 7 October 2026) to settle in. It must last your first weeks: plan your first-month budget.
  • Monthly transfers. Once you are enrolled in Italy, you send the enrolment papers to your bank in Tunisia, and your parents can then transfer up to 4,000 TND per month (about EUR 1,188 on the same date).

If you qualify, the DiSCo scholarship adds a yearly grant and free meals. See who can get the DiSCo scholarship.

7. What to do this month (October to December 2026)

  1. Pick two or three courses. Search on Universitaly and note each university's window and fee.
  2. Get your language certificate. Italian-taught courses: the university tests you at B2 level unless you already hold an Italian B2 certificate. English-taught courses: a B2 English certificate.
  3. Start legalising your documents. Begin with the ministry authentication of your bac and transcripts, the first step of the chain.
  4. Decide about LUISS. If it is on your list, apply on the LUISS admission page before 10 February 2027.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Tunisian student study in Italy for free?

Not completely. Public tuition can be low (Sapienza charges a flat EUR 300 a year for Tunisia's band) and the DiSCo scholarship can cover living costs if you qualify, but you still pay application, document and visa fees, and you must show the blocked amount for the visa.

How much money do I need to study in Rome?

For the visa, you must show EUR 10,179.85 (2026/27 figure), blocked at your bank. On top of that, budget for application fees, documents, the visa (EUR 50 + 68.300 TND), the residence permit (about EUR 116) and tuition, which ranges from EUR 300 to EUR 15,000 a year depending on the university.

When should I start preparing to study in Italy for 2027/28?

Now, in autumn 2026. Documents take months, LUISS applications close on 10 February 2027, and Sapienza's window is expected to open around December 2026.

Do I need to speak Italian to study in Rome?

Only for courses taught in Italian, where you need B2 level. Many Master's and some Bachelor's courses are taught in English; for those you need a B2 English certificate.

Does this guide cover Medicine in Italy?

Not yet. Italy reformed access to Medicine in 2025, and the rules for international students have not been published. We will add them once they are official.
