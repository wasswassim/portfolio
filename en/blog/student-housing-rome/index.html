Where will you live when you arrive in Rome, and how do you find a room without being scammed?

In short: the cheapest option is a DiSCo residence, which you can only request in the yearly DiSCo scholarship application. If you do not get one, use your university's housing service, then the rental platforms. Whatever you choose, see the room before paying, get a written contract and a receipt for every payment.

This is the last part of our guide to studying in Italy for Tunisian students. The earlier parts cover the application, the documents, the scholarship, the visa and your first steps in Rome. This part is about finding a place to live.

1. DiSCo residences: the cheapest option

DiSCo, the Lazio regional agency for the right to study, runs student residences in Rome. There is no separate application: you ask for a place in the same online application as the DiSCo scholarship, and the same income rules apply.

  • Cost: in 2026/27, about EUR 200-298 a month, deducted from your scholarship (EUR 700 is taken from the first instalment). See how the scholarship is paid.
  • When: places usually start from around mid-October, for up to 10 months (check the bando for your year).
  • Where: residences across Rome, for example Antonio Ruberti, Ezio Tarantelli, Falcone e Borsellino, Giulio Regeni, Valco San Paolo, Valleranello and Tor Vergata. The full list is on the DiSCo residences page↗ (opens in a new tab).
  • Timing: the 2026/27 housing results are already out. Plan for 2027/28: the application is expected around June-July 2027, with housing rankings around mid-September (check the 2027/28 call).

If you win a place, DiSCo sends a message to your personal area with the residence. Answer in time: refusing or not replying means you lose the place for that year. If you are eligible but not placed, the ranking moves automatically, usually about every two weeks, so keep checking your messages.

A residence is not proof for the visa

Applying for a DiSCo residence is not valid proof of accommodation for the visa. You need a hotel booking or a hospitality declaration for your first period in Italy (see the visa documents).

2. University housing services

The three big public universities in Rome do not guarantee you a room, but each one helps you look. Contact them as soon as you are admitted.

Sapienza

Open the Sapienza student housing page↗ (opens in a new tab). It lists the housing options and the Sturent desk, a service that helps students find rooms. Read the page before writing, so your questions are specific.

Tor Vergata

Open the Tor Vergata housing page↗ (opens in a new tab), then write to housing@uniroma2.it with your name, course and arrival date. Check the page for student discount codes on rental platforms.

Roma Tre

Roma Tre has a free accommodation service↗ (opens in a new tab). Write to accommodation@uniroma3.it to ask for help, and look on the page for platform discount codes.

Rents vary a lot by area and by month. Do not trust a figure you read online: check current listings for the neighbourhood near your faculty.

What to ask before you sign

  • Contract type: is it a written lease, and for how long? Ask for a contract that matches your study period.
  • Deposit: how much, and when exactly will it be returned?
  • Bills: are electricity, gas, water, internet and building fees included, or split between flatmates?
  • Registration: will the owner register the lease? You need a registered lease for many procedures.

A new lease must be registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate↗ (opens in a new tab) within 30 days. This is the owner's job, but ask for proof of registration and keep a copy. To sign a lease you may need a codice fiscale: you can get one at any Agenzia delle Entrate office (see how to get your codice fiscale).

Your first weeks: a temporary place

Many students book a hotel, a hostel or a short stay for the first weeks, then look for a room on the spot. This also solves the visa: you need proof of accommodation for the first period, either a hotel booking or a hospitality declaration from someone who hosts you. Visiting rooms in person is the safest way to choose.

4. How to avoid rental scams

Students who look from abroad are the easiest targets. The Polizia Postale's advice on rental scams↗ (opens in a new tab) comes down to one rule: see the room, or go through an authorised agency, and never pay in advance without guarantees.

  • See the room in person, or ask for a live video call where the owner shows the room and an ID document.
  • Never pay a deposit or rent before you have seen the room and the contract.
  • Refuse payment by Western Union, money transfer services, gift cards or crypto.
  • Get a written contract and a receipt for every payment.
  • Be wary of a rent far below the others in the same area, or an owner who is "abroad" and in a hurry.
  • Check that the owner's name matches the contract and the payment account.

If you have already paid

Stop all contact, keep every message and receipt, and report it to the police (Polizia Postale). Tell your university housing service too: it helps them warn other students.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a room in Rome as a student?

Start with the DiSCo residences (in the scholarship application), then your university's housing service, then platforms such as HousingAnywhere, Uniplaces, Spotahome, Immobiliare.it, Idealista and Subito. Many students book a short stay for the first weeks and visit rooms in person.

Do DiSCo residences cost money?

Yes, but little. In 2026/27 a place in Rome costs about EUR 200-298 a month, deducted from your DiSCo scholarship (EUR 700 comes from the first instalment).

Can I apply for a DiSCo residence without the scholarship?

No. You request the place in the same DiSCo application as the scholarship, with the same income rules and deadlines.

Do I need a codice fiscale to rent a room?

Usually yes: owners need it for the lease and its registration. You can get one at any Agenzia delle Entrate office with your passport and visa.

Does a DiSCo residence application count as accommodation proof for the visa?

No. For the visa you need a hotel booking or a hospitality declaration for your first period in Italy.

How do I avoid rental scams in Rome?

See the room or do a live video call, never pay before seeing it, refuse Western Union and gift cards, and get a contract and a receipt for every payment.
